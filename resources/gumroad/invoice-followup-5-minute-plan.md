# A five-minute system for overdue freelance invoices

*Free practical sample from Askoure Systems — no signup or purchase required.*

If an invoice has gone past its due date, avoid sending five variations of “just checking in.” First separate a payment administration problem from a disputed deliverable. A useful follow-up records the facts and asks for one clear next action; it does not threaten or assume bad intent.

## 1. Create one row for each open invoice

Copy these columns into a spreadsheet or a note:

`Client | Invoice ID | Amount/currency | Due date | Payment status | Last contact | Next action/date | Dispute/PO note`

Never put bank credentials, customer data, passwords or private invoice attachments in a public worksheet.

## 2. Check the basics before asking again

- Verify the invoice amount and due date from the signed agreement.
- Check that the client received the invoice and that the correct accounts-payable contact is copied.
- Confirm the PO reference or preferred invoice format if required.
- Separate “sent but waiting for AP” from “client disputes the scope.”

## 3. Use a concise first reminder

**Subject:** Invoice [ID] — payment status for [due date]

Hello [Name],

I am following up on invoice [ID] for [amount and currency], due [date], for [agreed project/deliverable]. Could you confirm receipt and the expected payment date? If your team needs a different PO reference or supporting document, please let me know.

Thank you,
[Your name]

## 4. If the client promises a date, track the promise

Replace an endless generic reminder with a specific question: “Thanks for the update. Can I record [date] as the expected payment date?” On that date, reconcile against actual cleared funds, not a screenshot, bank-transfer promise, or “paid” status inside another system.

If the client disputes the work, stop the routine reminder sequence: document the disputed item, the original acceptance criteria and the next decision needed. Repeating a payment demand will not resolve a scope disagreement.

## 5. Review only items requiring action

Once a day, filter for `Next action/date <= today`. Send a message only where a response is truly due, then record it. If no response arrives after a reasonable sequence, review the contract and locally applicable escalation options. This checklist is not legal advice, debt collection or a guarantee of payment.
