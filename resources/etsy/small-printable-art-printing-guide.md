# How to Print Small Digital Wall Art: 4×6 and 5×7 Inches

A practical, free printing guide for Moroccan-inspired digital wall art. You can use these steps with your own JPG prints; you do not need to buy anything.

## Choose the right crop, not just the right paper

| Frame opening | Shape | What to select |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 4 × 6 in (10.2 × 15.2 cm) | 2:3 portrait | The artwork file explicitly exported for **4×6** |
| 5 × 7 in (12.7 × 17.8 cm) | 5:7 portrait | The artwork file explicitly exported for **5×7** |

A 4×6 and a 5×7 frame do **not** have the same proportions. If you send a 4×6 image to a 5×7 lab print and choose “fill,” the lab may trim edges or crop decorative details. Choose the correctly labeled file first. For a visible white border, use the supplied bordered version when the seller provides one; for a print that reaches the paper edge, choose the cropped version when available.

## A five-step home-print checklist

1. **Download and unzip** the seller's original file. Keep the full-size JPG rather than printing a screenshot or a Pinterest preview.
2. **Select the exact paper size** (4×6 or 5×7 inches) in the print dialog.
3. **Choose portrait orientation** for a vertical artwork. Check the on-screen preview before confirming.
4. **Inspect “fit” versus “fill.”** *Fit* preserves the full design but may leave margins; *fill* may crop edges. A borderless setting can also trim a few millimetres, depending on the printer.
5. **Print one sample first** using reasonably good photo paper or a reliable local print shop. View it at ordinary reading distance before printing multiple copies.

For photo labs, ask whether the service offers true 4×6 / 5×7 paper and whether *auto-enhance* or *auto-crop* is enabled. Turn automatic cropping off where possible if the design includes important details close to the edges.

## Understand image sharpness

Image resolution and a file's declared PPI/DPI metadata are different things. Merely changing a file to “300 DPI” does **not** add actual image detail.

As a rough pixel-count reference, 4×6 inches at 300 pixels per inch would be 1200×1800 pixels, and 5×7 inches would be 1500×2100 pixels. These counts are **not** a guarantee of photographic detail or printer quality: original artwork detail, resampling, paper and ink matter too. Do not assume a small-format digital print will look sharp enlarged to a much bigger poster.

## Digital download or shipped print?

A **digital printable** is the file you receive. It normally does **not** include a physical frame, paper print, shipping service or personal printing support unless the listing explicitly promises those.
