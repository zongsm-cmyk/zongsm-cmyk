## Five easy wall-art combinations

### 1. A quiet blue accent beside a neutral reading chair

If your room already has beige, off-white or pale wood, choose a single **turquoise courtyard or blue door** accent for contrast. Repeat the color once with a cushion or book spine. Keep the rest of the wall relatively empty. This approach works especially well for compact nooks where a full gallery wall would feel crowded.

**Example style:** Moroccan courtyard, zellige-inspired fountain, warm stone walls.

### 2. A warm riad corner that doesn't need a renovation

For a little warmth in a plain lounge or rental, try one **Moroccan salon illustration** above a slim table, along with a textured throw. The goal is ambience without implying the print itself is a photograph or a hand-painted physical object.

**Example style:** carved arches, patterned cushions, amber room light.

### 3. Sunset color near a desk or hallway

A **Marrakech-inspired rooftop sunset** brings a soft orange-pink contrast to a simple office or entrance. A matte frame with a pale border can keep a small print looking intentional rather than lost on the wall.

**Example style:** rooftop view, lantern-like glow, warm evening sky.

### 4. Botanical kitchen art without food clutter

An **olive branch and pomegranate still-life illustration** can add a Mediterranean detail to a kitchen or breakfast nook. Pick a washable, protective frame or hang the art away from heat, steam, and splash zones.

**Example style:** olive leaves, red fruit, earthy ceramic textures.

### 5. One Moroccan blue door print as a conversation piece

Choose a bold **illustrated blue-green door**, not a mismatched group of identical generic posters. A single clear motif is often easier to style with furniture you already own.

**Example style:** teal archway and decorative stone or tile details.
